Human Activities Are Changing Our Climate

Discover how energy use, transport, deforestation, and everyday choices influence Earth’s climate—and what can be done about it.

By Climate & Clarity

The Climate Is Changing—and Human Activity Is a Major Cause

Earth’s climate has changed naturally throughout its history, but the rapid warming observed in recent decades is largely driven by human activities. Burning coal, oil, and gas for electricity, transport, and industry releases greenhouse gases that build up in the atmosphere and trap additional heat.

How Everyday Activities Affect the Planet

The choices we make are connected to larger systems. Cars and planes burn fossil fuels; power stations produce emissions when they generate electricity from coal or gas; and the production of food, clothing, cement, and other goods uses energy and resources. These activities add to the greenhouse gases warming the planet.

Deforestation Makes the Problem Worse

Trees absorb carbon dioxide as they grow and store carbon in their trunks, branches, roots, and soil. When forests are cleared or burned, that stored carbon can be released, and fewer trees remain to absorb carbon dioxide from the air. Protecting and restoring forests is therefore an important part of climate action.

What Climate Change Means for People and Nature

A warmer planet can increase the risk of intense heat, affect rainfall patterns, worsen some droughts and heavy-rain events, and contribute to rising sea levels as land ice melts and oceans warm. These changes can affect crops, wildlife habitats, water supplies, homes, and people’s health. The effects are not the same everywhere, and communities with fewer resources can face greater challenges.

Small Steps Matter—Alongside Bigger Changes

Individuals can help by wasting less food, using energy efficiently, walking or taking public transport when practical, repairing and reusing items, and choosing lower-emission options when available. But personal choices alone are not enough. Governments, businesses, and communities also need to expand clean energy, improve public transport, protect ecosystems, and reduce pollution across industries.

A Better Future Is Still Possible

Climate change is a serious challenge, but the future is not fixed. The faster societies reduce greenhouse-gas emissions and prepare for impacts already underway, the more harm can be limited. Learning the facts, discussing solutions, and supporting effective climate action can turn awareness into meaningful progress.
